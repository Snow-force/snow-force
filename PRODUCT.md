# Product

## Register

product

## Users

SnowForce serves Chinese snowboard and ski community members who want organized, trustworthy mountain activities. The primary audience includes first-time riders looking for approachable events, experienced riders looking for well-run trips and community sessions, and brand or resort partners evaluating SnowForce as an activity partner. Staff use the admin surface to keep bilingual event content, registrations, recaps, FAQs, and partner inquiries operational.

## Product Purpose

SnowForce helps riders discover current snow sports activities, understand whether an event fits their level, register without leaving the site, revisit past community recaps, and optionally maintain a lightweight member profile for future registrations. Success means public visitors can confidently choose an activity, members can reuse their details, partners can understand the community's energy, and staff can manage bilingual operations without editing code.

## Brand Personality

Vibrant, sporty, and youthful. The product should feel active and community-driven while staying credible enough for event logistics, registration, and partner conversations. The tone should be clear, energetic, bilingual, and welcoming to both beginners and experienced riders.

## Anti-references

SnowForce should not look too corporate, like a luxury resort brochure, like a generic SaaS dashboard, or like a childish cartoon sports brand. It should also avoid sterile Apple-clone minimalism when that makes the snow sports community feel distant or over-polished.

## Design Principles

1. Make the next ride feel reachable: event discovery and registration should reduce uncertainty for new and returning riders.
2. Balance energy with operational trust: public pages can feel lively, but registration and admin flows must stay clear and dependable.
3. Treat bilingual content as first-class: Chinese and English experiences should feel intentional rather than translated after the fact.
4. Show community proof through real activity: recaps, event details, and partner touchpoints should make SnowForce feel active in the mountains.
5. Keep staff workflows practical: admin screens should prioritize scanning, editing, publishing, and exporting over decorative presentation.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No special accessibility requirements are specified beyond strong product defaults. SnowForce should use responsive mobile-first layouts, readable bilingual copy, visible focus states, reduced-motion-friendly interactions, and color contrast suitable for public discovery, registration, and staff operations.
